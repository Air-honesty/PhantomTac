Abstract

Tactile perception is crucial for precise robotic manipulation and robotic arm grasping, providing an important foundation for dexterous, closed-loop force control. In this study, we discover a physical loophole called PhantomTac in magnetic tactile sensors. The attacker can alter the magnetic field, thus spoofing the force interpreted by magnetic tactile sensors.

The attacker can stealthily deploy such an attack by simply attaching a thin (the thickness is around 0.2 mm), battery-free magnetic tag to the surface of an object. Hence, the attack tag can be easily concealed by a barcode sticker. Such an attack scheme is "deploy-and-forget" - the attacker can deploy the tag and leave.

When a robotic hand is picking up an object, it may severely damage the object. Specifically, spoofing the normal force can crush the object, whereas shear force spoofing can lead to a loose grip, thus dropping the object. End-to-end evaluations demonstrate an attack success rate exceeding 95% with a grasping error of less than 8 mm.

Core Attack Vectors

—— A highly stealthy, passive physical-layer attack.

1. Normal Force Injection

Through the designed passive magnetic tags, the attacker injects spoofed normal force signals into the magnetic tactile sensor, causing the system to continuously receive erroneous contact force feedback.

2. Shear Force Spoofing

The attacker pre-injects spoofed shear force signals into the sensor and constructs steep spatial shear force gradient regions to counter dynamic algorithms.

3. High Stealthiness (NLOS)

PhantomTac utilizes micro-passive permanent magnets (with a thickness of 0.5 mm). The magnetic field can penetrate non-ferromagnetic materials, allowing tags to be hidden under normal labels.

Q & A

Q1: Which tactile sensors are affected by this vulnerability?

A: We conducted extensive reverse engineering research on commercial COTS magnetic tactile sensors (such as PX6AX-GEN3-MC-M2020-Elite and Xela uSPa 46).

Q2: Can existing visual monitoring detect these tags?

A: It is highly difficult. The maliciously deployed magnetic tags can effectively blend with conventional legitimate labels (like barcode stickers) in terms of visual features. We selected miniature magnets with a thickness of 0.5 mm , which causes almost no changes to the visual appearance of the target object.
